------------------------------------------------------------------
0. WHAT IT IS
------------------------------------------------------------------
The atlas node in your pocket: a phone app that is (a) a mesh member,
(b) a Lightning wallet holding your walk/hosting earnings, and (c) a
sovereign spend rail — tap phone-to-phone or phone-to-tag and pay in
sats directly, no card networks, no banks, no app stores. We use the
HARDWARE (NFC, secure element, camera) and run our OWN software on
it, distributed over Tor and verified against Bitcoin. A small
network fee on each spend feeds the epoch pool. Earn sats by
thinking (walks) or hosting (custody); spend them on coffee; the
fee funds the next prize. The economy closes its own loop and no
outside party can turn any part of it off.

------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE PHONE NODE (honest tiering)
------------------------------------------------------------------
The 42 MB pure-Rust node (arti Tor included) compiles for ARM. But
phones sleep, and iOS kills background sockets. So the pocket node is
a MEMBER, not a CARRIER:

  T1.1  MEMBER (phone): holds your keys, signs your walks
        (pow_pubkey/pow_sig from PROOF_OF_WALK §2), receives LN
        payouts, answers custody challenges only for content it
        explicitly pinned, participates in gossip WHEN OPEN.
        Earns: walk tickets, draw prizes, spend cashback (§4).
  T1.2  CARRIER (always-on box): hosts, serves, earns rent per the
        bandwidth economy. A phone never pretends to be one — no
        uptime lies, no slashing risk for sleeping.
  T1.3  A member phone can PAIR with a carrier they own (home box,
        VPS): one identity, two devices, the carrier earns while
        the phone sleeps. Pairing = both keys co-sign a link event,
        gossiped like everything else.

------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE SPEND RAIL (sovereign — we go around the card networks)
------------------------------------------------------------------
v0 routed spending through Apple Pay / Google Pay. DELETED in v0.2:
cards mean an issuer, a BIN sponsor, and KYC — a permanent admin key
over the whole economy. Instead we use the HARDWARE (NFC radio,
secure element, camera) and build our OWN software on it.

  R2.1  ATLAS PAY, phone-to-phone: the merchant runs the same app.
        Customer taps the merchant's phone (or any NFC tag / QR
        code) — the tap carries a signed LN/BOLT12 payment request,
        the customer's app pays it from their sat balance over
        Lightning. Settlement is instant, final, and fee-light.
        No issuer, no PAN, no KYC, no chargebacks. Android's HCE
        (host-card-emulation) API emits raw NDEF over NFC without
        any card network's permission — this is proven tech (Bolt
        Card / LNURL-over-NFC), not research.
  R2.2  The merchant's "terminal" is a phone with the app in
        register mode, or a $10 NFC tag on the counter (static
        BOLT12 offer — reusable, amount typed by the customer).
        Acceptance costs a merchant NOTHING but the app.
  R2.3  The balance is the bearer-ticket/LN wallet from BW §4 —
        one balance earns (walks, custody) and spends (taps).
        The mesh never holds fiat. There is no fiat.
  R2.4  TWO-SIDED HONESTY: this rail only works where the MERCHANT
        opted in. You cannot tap a legacy Visa terminal with it —
        that is the price of zero chokepoints, stated plainly.
        Users who want legacy-rail convenience can sweep sats to
        any third-party card product THEY choose; that bridge lives
        entirely outside the mesh and is never a dependency.
  R2.5  LAW: no mesh data structure ever links spending identity to
        node identity. Payments are LN/ecash-native; the merchant
        learns an invoice was paid, nothing else.

------------------------------------------------------------------
2b. DISTRIBUTION (upload from Tor, not from the stores)
------------------------------------------------------------------
The app never touches Google Play or the App Store. The stores are
a kill switch and a surveillance tax; we decline both.

  D2b.1 The APK is served from the mesh itself: an onion service
        (atlas nodes can pin it per BW §3 — the app is just pinned
        content), plus a clearnet git mirror for discoverability.
  D2b.2 REPRODUCIBLE BUILDS: anyone can build the APK from source
        and get the byte-identical artifact. The release hash is a
        settlement leaf — every release is inscribed under the
        anchor on Bitcoin. An APK whose hash isn't anchored is not
        ours, full stop.
  D2b.3 UPDATES OVER NOSTR: releases are announced as signed Nostr
        events (kind TBD) carrying version, hash, and onion fetch
        URLs. The app checks its own update feed over Tor. No
        store, no CDN, no phone-home. The mesh distributes itself.
  D2b.4 Android first-class (sideload is a checkbox; GrapheneOS/
        CalyxOS users are the natural early adopters). iOS stated
        honestly: Apple forbids NFC freedom + sideloading in most
        jurisdictions — iPhone users get QR-only via web/PWA until
        regulation or jailbreak-free paths open. We do not contort
        the design to please Cupertino.

------------------------------------------------------------------
2c. THE MERCHANT SIDE (the cold-start engine — added v0.3)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Merchants aren't just acceptance points; they're the second revenue
engine and the customer-acquisition loop, in the SAME app:

  M2c.1 REGISTER MODE + DIRECTORY: any merchant flips the app into
        register mode and gets a listing in the mesh-merchant
        directory (gossiped like all content). Walkers see who
        accepts nearby. Listing is free forever.
  M2c.2 MERCHANTS BUY DRAW TICKETS: a merchant purchases tickets
        from the epoch pool (sats in -> pool grows) and hands them
        out at point of purchase or via geofenced drops. This is
        their advertising budget — and every sat of it lands in
        the pool that pays walkers. Ad spend IS pool revenue: the
        network's second engine beside the fee (§3).
  M2c.3 GEOFENCED DROPS, PRIVACY LAW: geofence evaluation happens
        ON DEVICE. The customer's phone decides "am I inside the
        drop zone"; the mesh never learns a location, the merchant
        learns only aggregate redeemed-count. No location ever
        appears in any mesh data structure.
  M2c.4 DUAL LANE: default lane is NON-KYC pure sats — sovereign,
        nothing to ask anyone. Merchants who need fiat on their
        books can OPTIONALLY connect a third-party KYC'd sweep
        partner (auto-convert sats->bank, their choice of vendor).
        That bridge lives entirely outside the mesh (mirrors R2.4)
        and is never a dependency. KYC never touches the mesh.
  M2c.5 WHY GOOGLE CAN'T BLOCK THIS: HCE/NFC APIs are AOSP —
        Android itself, not Play Services. Sideloaded apps get
        full radio access, and we never enter the Play Store so
        their policies never bind us. Worst case: a Play Protect
        install warning (cosmetic; absent on Graphene/Calyx).
        Apple CAN block native NFC (entitlement-gated outside
        DMA regions) — hence D2b.4's iOS honesty clause stands.
        Merchant terminals are Android phones or tags; no
        merchant ever needs Cupertino's permission.

3. THE NETWORK FEE (the engine — was "FX spread", now simpler)
------------------------------------------------------------------
With no fiat conversion there is no FX. The pool feed becomes an
honest, visible NETWORK FEE on spends:

  F3.1  fee f is CONTINUOUS and public: f = f_base * v(t), v(t)
        adjusting smoothly with epoch pool health (pool fat = fee
        compresses toward zero; pool thin = widens slightly).
        Target f_base ~0.5-1%. Printed on every payment preview —
        the payer approves it with the payment, every time.
  F3.2  Split per spend, all as settlement leaves under the anchor:
          - LN routing cost: whatever it actually costs (usually
            near zero)
          - epoch pool: the remainder (prizes, custody payouts,
            anchor fees — the same pool everything else feeds)
          - spend cashback: a sliver back to the spender in draw
            tickets (spending = participation; see A5.3 before
            you love this)
  F3.3  Every epoch's total fee revenue is merkled and inscribed.
        Anyone can audit what the network skimmed and where it went.

4. WHY ANYONE USES IT (the loop, stated plainly)
------------------------------------------------------------------
  Walk the dream -> earn sats. Host content -> earn sats. Tap a
  friend's phone, a market stall, a coffee counter that joined ->
  a sub-1% whisper feeds the pool that pays the next walker. Your
  coffee funds open research and the infrastructure that hosts it,
  and you can VERIFY every hop of that claim on Bitcoin. No ads, no
  token, no data sale, no bank in the loop — the network's only
  revenue is service people chose to buy, on rails no one can
  revoke. Merchants join because acceptance is FREE (an app or a
  $10 tag), settlement is instant and final, and 0.5-1% beats the
  2-3% the card networks take today.

5. KNOWN OPEN ATTACKS (start here, demolishers)
------------------------------------------------------------------
  A5.1  MERCHANT COLD-START: §2c is the designed answer (free
        acceptance + directory + ticket-ads as acquisition). Attack
        it: do ticket giveaways actually pull customers, or is it
        two-sided-market wishful thinking? What density of merchants
        per km² makes the directory worth opening? Is organic
        LN-merchant overlap (BTCMap ~7k+ venues) a seed lane?
  A5.1b GEOFENCE SPOOFING: M2c.3 evaluates drops on-device — a
        rooted phone fakes GPS and farms every geofenced ticket
        drop in the city from a couch. Rate-limits per pubkey,
        PoW cost per claim, NFC-tap-to-claim (physical presence
        proof beats GPS), or accept the leak as marketing cost?
  A5.2  FEE TIMING ARBITRAGE: v(t) is public and continuous — can a
        spender time spends against pool health, and does that hurt
        anything? (Maybe it's fine: gaming the fee = using the
        network.)
  A5.3  CASHBACK WASH-SPENDING: draw tickets for spending invites
        buy-refund loops and self-merchant spins to farm tickets.
        Caps? Net-of-refund accounting windows? Or delete cashback
        entirely (simplest fix — does §4's loop survive without it)?
  A5.4  PHONE KEY CUSTODY: the pocket key signs walks AND spends.
        Phone theft = identity + balance. Secure-enclave sigs,
        spending sub-keys with limits, carrier-pair as recovery?
  A5.5  IDENTITY BLEED: R2.5 promises spend identity never links to
        node identity, but both live in one app on one device.
        Traffic analysis on LN routes, NFC session correlation,
        OS-level telemetry on stock Android — enumerate the leak
        paths and state which are survivable (and which require
        GrapheneOS to survive).
  A5.6  MALICIOUS UPDATE FEED: D2b.3 makes Nostr events the update
        channel. Compromise of the release signing key = malware on
        every node. Does the update event need M-of-N release
        signatures (treasury-style) + a mandatory anchor-inscription
        check before install? (Probably yes — spec it.)
  A5.7  TAG SWAPPING: a $10 static NFC tag on a counter can be
        peeled and replaced by a thief's tag. Merchant app should
        show expected-offer fingerprint; customers see merchant
        name in the payment preview — is that enough, or do tags
        need to die in favor of register-mode only?
  A5.8  Every constant. f_base, splits, cashback sliver — guesses.
        Attack with mechanism, not taste.
