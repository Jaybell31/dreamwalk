------------------------------------------------------------------
0. WHAT IT IS
------------------------------------------------------------------
The atlas node in your pocket: a phone app that is (a) a mesh member,
(b) a Lightning wallet holding your walk/hosting earnings, and (c) a
spend rail — tap your phone at any store that takes Apple Pay or
Google Pay and pay from your sats. A small FX spread on each spend
feeds the epoch pool. Earn sats by thinking (walks) or hosting
(custody); spend them on coffee; the spread funds the next prize.
The economy closes its own loop.

------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE PHONE NODE (honest tiering)
------------------------------------------------------------------
The 42 MB pure-Rust node (arti Tor included) compiles for ARM. But
phones sleep, and iOS kills background sockets. So the pocket node is
a MEMBER, not a CARRIER:

  T1.1  MEMBER (phone): holds your keys, signs your walks
        (pow_pubkey/pow_sig from PROOF_OF_WALK §2), receives LN
        payouts, answers custody challenges only for content it
        explicitly pinned, participates in gossip WHEN OPEN.
        Earns: walk tickets, draw prizes, spend cashback (§4).
  T1.2  CARRIER (always-on box): hosts, serves, earns rent per the
        bandwidth economy. A phone never pretends to be one — no
        uptime lies, no slashing risk for sleeping.
  T1.3  A member phone can PAIR with a carrier they own (home box,
        VPS): one identity, two devices, the carrier earns while
        the phone sleeps. Pairing = both keys co-sign a link event,
        gossiped like everything else.

------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE SPEND RAIL (where honesty is mandatory)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Apple Pay / Google Pay acceptance means a CARD gets provisioned into
the phone's wallet. Cards mean an ISSUER, a BIN sponsor, and KYC at
the fiat boundary. There is no permissionless path through Visa/MC —
anyone who claims otherwise is selling something.

  R2.1  The mesh side stays sovereign: sats live in the user's own
        LN/ecash balance (bearer tickets from BW §4 double as the
        spend balance). The ISSUER never custodies idle sats.
  R2.2  At tap time: spend amount converts sat->fiat at spot + a
        continuous FX spread f (target ~1%, see §3), the issuer
        settles the merchant in fiat, the user's sats debit over LN
        in real time. Strike/Fold/Pouch proved this flow works.
  R2.3  The issuer is a PLUGGABLE, REPLACEABLE partner — the app
        ships with N issuer integrations, the user picks, and the
        mesh survives any issuer dying or defecting. The chokepoint
        is acknowledged, contained, and interchangeable. KYC scope
        is the card only, never the node identity: pow_pubkey and
        card identity are never linked in any mesh data structure.
  R2.4  LAW: the mesh NEVER holds fiat, never touches the card PAN,
        never learns what you bought. Purchase metadata dies at the
        issuer boundary (their regulatory burden, not our data).

------------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE FX SPREAD (the engine)
------------------------------------------------------------------
  F3.1  spread f is CONTINUOUS and public: f = f_base * v(t), where
        v(t) adjusts smoothly with epoch pool health (pool fat =
        spread compresses toward cost; pool thin = widens slightly).
        No tiers, no hidden markup — f is printed on every receipt.
  F3.2  Split per spend, all as settlement leaves under the anchor:
          - issuer/processing cost: whatever it actually costs
          - epoch pool: the remainder (prizes, custody payouts,
            anchor fees — the same pool everything else feeds)
          - spend cashback: a sliver back to the spender in draw
            tickets (spending = participation; see A5.3 before
            you love this)
  F3.3  Every epoch's total FX revenue is merkled and inscribed.
        Anyone can audit what the network skimmed and where it went.

------------------------------------------------------------------
4. WHY ANYONE USES IT (the loop, stated plainly)
------------------------------------------------------------------
  Walk the dream -> earn sats. Host content -> earn sats. Spend sats
  at any card terminal on earth -> a ~1% whisper feeds the pool that
  pays the next walker. Your coffee funds open research and the
  infrastructure that hosts it, and you can VERIFY every hop of that
  claim on Bitcoin. No ads, no token, no data sale — the network's
  only revenue is service people chose to buy.

5. KNOWN OPEN ATTACKS (start here, demolishers)
------------------------------------------------------------------
  A5.1  ISSUER CAPTURE: the replaceable-partner story (R2.3) is only
        as good as the number of live integrations. One issuer = a
        de facto admin key over spending. What's the minimum N, and
        does the app need a Bolt-Card/LN-native fallback lane that
        skips card rails entirely at LN-accepting merchants?
  A5.2  FX SPREAD ARBITRAGE: v(t) is public and continuous — can a
        spender time spends against pool health, and does that hurt
        anything? (Maybe it's fine: gaming the spread = using the
        network.)
  A5.3  CASHBACK WASH-SPENDING: draw tickets for spending invites
        buy-refund loops and self-merchant spins to farm tickets.
        Caps? Net-of-refund accounting windows? Or delete cashback
        entirely (simplest fix — does §4's loop survive without it)?
  A5.4  PHONE KEY CUSTODY: the pocket key signs walks AND spends.
        Phone theft = identity + balance. Secure-enclave sigs,
        spending sub-keys with limits, carrier-pair as recovery?
  A5.5  KYC BLEED: R2.3 promises card identity never links to node
        identity, but both live in one app on one device. Traffic
        analysis, issuer subpoena, app-store telemetry — enumerate
        the leak paths and state which are survivable.
  A5.6  Every constant. f_base, splits, cashback sliver — guesses.
        Attack with mechanism, not taste.
