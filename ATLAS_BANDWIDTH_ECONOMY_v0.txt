------------------------------------------------------------------
0. THE PROBLEM
------------------------------------------------------------------
When people start HOSTING on the mesh — onion services, mirrors, graph
windows, dreamscape maps — someone's disk, CPU, and uplink carries it.
Volunteer bandwidth collapses exactly when growth arrives (every P2P
network's graveyard). The mesh needs hosting to PAY THE NETWORK, without
introducing accounts, admins, or surveillance.

One honesty clause up front: atlas nodes gossip OVER Tor, and the public
Tor relay network itself is unpaid by design — we can't pay Tor and don't
pretend to. What we price is ATLAS-layer service: storage, replication,
and serving done by atlas nodes for atlas users.

------------------------------------------------------------------
1. FIRST LAW: PRICE SERVICE, NEVER METER BANDWIDTH
------------------------------------------------------------------
Metered-bandwidth rewards die by self-dealing: a node inflates "bytes
served" receipts by serving itself through proxies. Decades of P2P
incentive papers, one graveyard.

  L1.1  No sat is ever minted for CLAIMED traffic. Sats flow only where
        a customer's payment already happened. A byte is worth exactly
        what someone paid to move it; fake traffic is self-funded and
        therefore harmless.
  L1.2  Corollary: there is no "bandwidth subsidy pool" to farm. The
        pool is revenue, and revenue requires a payer.

------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE TWO SERVICES
------------------------------------------------------------------
  PINNING  : host pays for their content to EXIST on the mesh —
             replicated, gossiped, alive while they sleep.
  SERVING  : reader pays (usually nothing — see free tier) for bytes
             delivered on request.

------------------------------------------------------------------
3. PINNING (rent, paid to the epoch pool)
------------------------------------------------------------------
  P3.1  A host announces a PIN CONTRACT: (content_hash, size_bytes,
        replication_r, epochs_e) and pays rent to the treasury over LN.
  P3.2  Rent is a CONTINUOUS function (no tiers, no buckets):

          rent(size, r, e) = k * size * r * e * u(t)

        where u(t) = exp(load/capacity of the mesh at time t), a
        congestion multiplier recomputed each epoch from settled
        pin totals vs advertised capacity. Idle mesh = cheap rent;
        full mesh = rent rises smoothly until someone adds nodes.
        (Adding capacity is how you short the rent curve — the
        incentive to grow the mesh is IN the price.)
  P3.3  Nodes that carry the pin earn the rent pro-rata per epoch,
        SPLIT BY PROOF OF CUSTODY: each epoch the anchor_id seeds a
        random challenge (byte-range hash of the pinned content ||
        anchor_id); a carrying node answers in its settlement claim.
        Can't answer = didn't store it = no share. The challenge is
        deterministic from the anchor, so it's free to verify and
        impossible to precompute before the epoch closes.
  P3.4  Pin contracts, custody answers, and payouts are settlement
        leaves — merkled into the kind-31340 anchor, inscribed on
        Bitcoin. Rent history is as tamper-proof as walk history.

------------------------------------------------------------------
4. SERVING (prepaid bearer tickets, ecash-style)
------------------------------------------------------------------
  S4.1  Readers who want priority service buy BEARER TICKETS over LN:
        blind-signed tokens (Cashu-style) denominated in sats, issued
        against the treasury. Blind signing = the mesh cannot link a
        ticket to the wallet that bought it. Reader privacy survives
        payment. (Court-blindness ethos, applied to bandwidth.)
  S4.2  A request arrives with a ticket attached; the serving node
        redeems the ticket in its epoch settlement claim. Redemption
        is the receipt — no traffic logs, no metering, nothing to
        fake. L1.1 satisfied by construction.
  S4.3  FREE TIER IS LAW: unpaid requests are still served best-effort
        (rate-limited), tickets buy PRIORITY not ACCESS. The open door
        (walks, graph reads, verdicts) is never paywalled. Knowledge
        stays free; convenience pays the bills.

------------------------------------------------------------------
5. WHERE THE MONEY GOES
------------------------------------------------------------------
  M5.1  All rent + ticket revenue lands in the epoch pool alongside
        (eventually replacing) the seeded prize funds. Split per
        epoch, continuous weights, no buckets:
          - carrying/serving nodes: pro-rata by custody proofs +
            redeemed tickets
          - proof-of-walk draw: fixed fraction (the door that brings
            new minds stays funded)
          - treasury retention: fixed fraction for the next epoch's
            LN liquidity + on-chain anchor fees
  M5.2  Every split is a settlement leaf under the anchor. Anyone can
        audit an epoch's revenue and payouts against the chain.

------------------------------------------------------------------
6. KNOWN OPEN ATTACKS (start here, demolishers)
------------------------------------------------------------------
  A6.1  CUSTODY OUTSOURCING: N "carriers" back-end to one disk and
        split the challenge answers. r-of-N replication becomes 1-of-1
        with extra steps. Does the challenge need latency bounds or
        per-node salts (encrypt each replica to the carrier's key)?
  A6.2  TICKET MINT CUSTODY: blind-signing against the treasury makes
        the 2-of-3 a MINT — ecash liabilities against a multisig.
        What's the run-risk story? Cap outstanding float per epoch?
  A6.3  FREE-TIER STARVATION: can paid traffic crowd out the open door
        entirely on a congested mesh? Does S4.3 need a reserved-
        capacity floor written into settlement rules?
  A6.4  RENT GRIEFING: pin garbage at scale to inflate u(t) and price
        rivals out. Self-funded per L1.1 — but is the congestion
        multiplier manipulable more cheaply than the damage it does?
  A6.5  EXIT-SCAM PINS: host prepays 100 epochs, mesh drops the content
        at epoch 50. Slashing? Escrowed rent released per-epoch? (Rent
        already streams per-epoch in P3.3 — is that enough?)
  A6.6  LN LIQUIDITY: nightly micro-payouts need inbound liquidity at
        every earning node. Who funds channels? Is ticket redemption
        better settled in ecash (carry a balance) than raw LN?
  A6.7  Every constant in §3-§5. All numbers are guesses. Attack with
        mechanism, not taste.
